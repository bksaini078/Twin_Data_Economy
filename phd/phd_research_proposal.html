01 · Introduction

The Data Exhaustion Crisis and Synthetic Methodologies

The proliferation of Large Language Models (LLMs) has precipitated a structural shift in how behavioral data is generated and consumed. As AI-generated material increasingly saturates the active web corpus (estimated at 30% to 40%), the risk of a model collapse loop (where models train on synthetic artifacts rather than ground-truth human behavior) has become acute. Concurrently, behavioral research faces a fidelity crisis, as traditional methods struggle to capture the nuance of diverse human populations at scale. This research addresses the critical need for scientifically grounded, high-fidelity human data pipelines in the age of generative AI.

02 · Motivation

Methodological Flaws in Current Behavioral Data Paradigms

Current paradigms for behavioral modeling and market simulation exhibit significant methodological and ethical vulnerabilities.

1. Non-Consensual Extraction

Platforms harvest behavioral signals without explicit user consent. Empirical data indicates that approximately 74% to 82% of consumers report feeling a loss of control over their data. This paradigm is increasingly untenable under tightening global regulatory frameworks.

2. Shallow Market Research

Traditional survey methodologies rely on generic, static personas and inherently small sample sizes. They systematically fail to capture the longitudinal dynamics and nuanced variance of real human behavior.

3. The Fidelity Gap

While synthetic data generation using LLMs is accelerating, empirical evidence indicates that without rich psychological grounding, synthetic participants exhibit substantial alignment gaps and heuristic biases compared to their human counterparts (Bisbee et al., 2024).

03 · Vision

A Consensual, Participatory Data Architecture

This research proposes a paradigm shift toward a participatory behavioral data architecture. Instead of covert extraction, the model relies on explicit consent and data ownership.

By empowering individuals to own and selectively provision access to their digital twins (constructed via privacy-preserving, longitudinal embeddings of their authentic micro-behaviors), we can generate high-fidelity, auditable synthetic panels. This approach theoretically aligns methodological rigor with ethical data sovereignty, allowing researchers to query behavioral models at scale while ensuring that the humans who supply the foundational ground-truth data retain control.

04 · Related Work

Synthetic Data and Agentic Simulation Landscape

The rapidly evolving landscape of synthetic respondents is characterized by varying methodological approaches and degrees of human grounding. Recent academic literature has highlighted severe systematic limitations in uncalibrated LLM persona simulations.

Approach / Typology Notable Research & Literature Methodological Limitations & Findings
Systematic Critiques of Synthetic Data Bisbee et al. (2024); Kuric et al. (2026) Highlight the perils of LLMs as survey replacements, citing heuristic biases, cognitive misalignments, and conditional fidelity. Emphasize the need for rigorous persona construction.
Distortion in Digital Twins Peng et al. (2026) Identify five systematic distortions in LLM-based digital twins: insufficient individuation, stereotyping, representation bias, ideological bias, and hyper-rationality.
Retrieval-Augmented Personas Jia et al. (2026) Demonstrate that digital twins built on survey histories reliably approximate human responses for stable traits, but struggle with high-variance or subjective queries.
Conversational Synthetic Commercial frameworks (e.g., Synthetic Users) Focus on qualitative interviews and open-ended concept validation. Often lack deep longitudinal grounding, risking generic or homogenized responses.
Population-Grounded Simulation Commercial frameworks (e.g., Ditto, Simile AI) Target large-scale segmentation and society simulation. While achieving massive scale, empirical validation of individual-level alignment against specific subgroups remains an open challenge.
05 · Research Questions

Core Research Questions

The thesis is structured around three research questions spanning agentic AI system design, simulation fidelity, platform validation, and the emerging twin data economy. RQ1 covers the full arc from agentic AI requirements to simulation distortion and mitigation (anchored by Papers 1–5 and P1). RQ2 addresses platform requirements gathering and empirical evaluation against traditional approaches (P2). RQ3 examines business value, enterprise use cases, and governance in the twin data economy (P3).

06 · System Architecture

Twinlytics Platform

Twinlytics is the software artifact developed as part of this research. It implements a four-stage human digital twin pipeline. The platform serves as the primary testbed for all empirical evaluations in the thesis.
